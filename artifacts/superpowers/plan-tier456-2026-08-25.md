## What the 2026 research and the measurements changed

### F-1 — We have the first half of the standard retrieval pipeline and none of the second

The 2026 consensus shape is **two stages**: hybrid dense+sparse retrieval fused
with RRF for recall, then a **reranker** for precision. `scout/backends/pgvector.py`
implements stage one exactly — `ROW_NUMBER()` over `embedding <=>` and over
`ts_rank(tsv, plainto_tsquery(...))`, fused with `1/(60+rank)`. There is no
stage two.

The literature describes cross-encoder reranking as *"the cheapest,
highest-leverage fix for RAG systems where vector search returns the right
answer somewhere in the top 50 but rarely at rank 1"*. That is **precisely the
symptom measured on this system** during the status check: for hint
`"Convolutional Neural Networks"` against the page that cites it, rank 1 was a
bibliography fragment (`ACM 2017, 60, 84–90. [CrossRef] 50. Hinton, G.E. Deep
belief networks`) and the actual CNN section came back at rank 2.

### F-2 — The bibliography is 18% of the index, and it is *both* the noise and T4.2's raw material

Measured, not estimated:

| Measurement | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Parsed text that is the reference list | **21.1%** (chars 86,415–109,457) |
| Numbered reference entries | **87** — the same 87 T4.2 names |
| Indexed chunks matching `[CrossRef]` / `doi.org` / `[PubMed]` | **23 of 127 (18%)** |

So roughly one chunk in five is a reference-list entry, and reference entries are
*dense with exact paper titles* — which is why BM25 puts them first for a query
that is itself a paper title. This is not a tuning problem; it is 18% of the
corpus being a different kind of object indexed as if it were prose.

**The consequence for the plan is the most useful thing in it:** the reference
list is simultaneously the noise polluting retrieval (Tier 5) and the raw
material for the citation graph (T4.2). Extracting it into structured citations
**once** serves both — it removes 23 noise chunks at zero model cost and
produces the 87 edges T4.2 needs. One change, two tiers, no reranker required
to get most of the benefit.

### F-3 — PDF extraction moved on, which reframes T5.1's decision

T5.1 poses a binary: add `pdfplumber` + `pypdf[image]`, or decline and say so.
The 2026 landscape offers a third answer. **Docling** (IBM Research) is the
production-RAG choice — an RT-DETR layout model trained on DocLayNet plus
TableFormer for table structure, CPU-viable, emitting a structured document that
preserves semantic hierarchy. The guidance is explicit: `pdfplumber` for
grid-line tables in simple PDFs; Docling or Unstructured *"when feeding an LLM
pipeline and needing structured, chunk-ready output"* — which is this system.

This matters beyond figures: a layout-aware parser identifies the reference
section *as a section*, which is exactly what F-2 needs. It also carries a real
cost — a much larger dependency and a wider parser attack surface (SH-7).

### F-4 — GraphRAG practice answers T4.2's open question

T4.2 asks: does each reference become a page, or a graph edge on the citing
page? Current GraphRAG systems answer it with **distinct node types** —
`Paper`, `CitationPaper`, `Chunk` — and distinct edges (`CITES` within the
corpus, `CITES_PAPER` outward), plus `ALIAS_OF` for entity resolution. The
practical reading for this repository: an outward reference is **not** a wiki
page. It is metadata on the citing page until the cited work is itself ingested
into `raw/`, at which point it becomes a real `[[wikilink]]`. That respects
R-1.5 (relations are wikilinks in the body, never a `related:` field) without
minting 87 stub pages that cite nothing and answer nothing.

The other applicable finding: bounded **seed schemas** beat open SPO extraction
for schema consistency. A reference is a closed shape (authors, title, venue,
year, doi) and should be parsed, not prompted.

### Goal

The corpus is honest about what it contains, retrieval returns the right chunk
first, the vault stops being a sample of five, and the default test command
reports green. Concretely:

1. No status field reports success for a capability the code did not exercise.
2. The reference list is structured citation data, not 18% of the retrievable
   prose — and rank 1 for a page's own hint is that page's own section.
3. The remaining 10 planned articles are compiled **against the cleaned corpus**,
   not the polluted one.
4. `uv run pytest` reports green on a machine with no live stack.
5. Every deferred decision is either taken or recorded with its cost.

Non-goals: ingesting new source documents; Word/Excel parsers (SH-8 is a
separate epic); building a general GraphRAG engine; anything requiring a new
paid provider.

### Assumptions

- **A1.** Re-indexing costs embedding calls only (Gemini `snp-embed`), not
  judge or LLM calls. 127 chunks is well inside limits. Verified: the ingest
  path embeds; it does not generate.
- **A2.** The 5 existing wiki pages' `sources[]` hints still resolve after the
  bibliography is removed from the index. **Step 8 verifies this before anything
  depends on it** — `verify-addresses` currently reports 5/5 PASS, and that must
  still hold afterwards or the change is reverted.
- **A3.** `snp-judge` works. Verified live: 5 pages judged GROUNDED, exit 0. But
  it runs on an OpenRouter free key with a **50 requests/day** ceiling, so
  compiling 10 articles (each generating and judging) is budget-sensitive and
  belongs in one deliberate run, not in a loop.
- **A4.** T4.3 (`derived/`) stays deferred. It is the only blocker on `extract`,
  and it needs a decision this plan cannot take — see **Decisions needed**.
- **A5.** No reranker is added in this plan. F-2 says most of the observed
  precision loss is 18% noise, which is removable structurally at zero cost.
  Step 9 measures whether a reranker is still needed **after** that, so the
  decision is made on evidence rather than on the literature.

### Risks & mitigations

- **R1 — Removing 18% of the index could break the 5 existing addresses.** This
  is the plan's central risk and A2 names it. Mitigation: step 8's verification
  is a hard gate — `verify-addresses` must stay 5/5 PASS and groundedness 5
  GROUNDED, or the step reverts. The addresses were minted against a corpus that
  *included* the bibliography, so this is a real possibility, not a formality.
- **R2 — Compiling 10 articles costs real money on a 50/day free key.** Each
  article generates twice and judges once. Mitigation: it is one deliberate run,
  last, after the corpus is clean; check remaining budget first; the staging
  directory and Tier 2's resume mean a budget exhaustion mid-run is resumable
  rather than lost.
- **R3 — Compiling on the cleaned corpus may produce different prose than the 5
  existing pages.** They were compiled against the polluted index. Mitigation:
  accept the inconsistency and record it; recompiling the 5 to match would
  double the cost for a cosmetic gain. If it matters, it is a separate decision.
- **R4 — Reference parsing is regex-shaped and this is one document.** 87 entries
  from one publisher's style. Mitigation: the parser reports what it could not
  parse rather than silently dropping it — the entire point of Phase B. A second
  document with a different style is a known future gap, recorded not hidden.
- **R5 — `ruff format` on 90 files could hide a real change.** Mitigation: its
  own commit, nothing else in flight, and a verification that no non-whitespace
  content changed.
- **R6 — Phase B changes a status value downstream code may branch on.**
  `figures_status: "ok"` → `"unavailable"` is a behaviour change for anything
  reading it. Mitigation: grep every consumer first; the whole reason to change
  it is that the current value is false.

### Rollback plan

- **Phase A** — independent and additive. Each step reverts alone.
- **Phase B** — status values and a linter finding. Reverting restores the
  (dishonest) `ok`; no data changes.
- **Phase C** — steps 7 and 10 are additive. **Step 8 changes what is indexed**,
  and is the only step requiring a re-index to undo: revert the chunker change
  and re-run ingestion to restore all 127 chunks. No source file is modified —
  `raw/` is read-only (R-3.1) throughout.
- **Phase D** — step 12 writes 10 new pages. `git revert` removes them; the
  staging directory makes a partial run resumable rather than wasted.
- **Phase E** — formatting only.

Nothing in this plan modifies `raw/`, and nothing deletes a wiki page.
